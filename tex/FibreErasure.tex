  \begin{abstract}
    We establish a fibre-erasure theorem for the admissible coarse-graining hierarchy
    of the Cosmochrony spectral programme~\cite{Beau2026l,Beau2026m}.
    The non-injective projection $\Pi : \Omegaspace \to \Obs$ defines a residual
    fibre-information functional $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$,
    where $c$ is a Weil-block fibre label and $\sigma_c(\ell)$ is the BFS capacity
    profile at coarse-graining depth~$\ell$.
    The structural sufficiency hypothesis~\textup{[H-suff]} ---
    that $\sigma(\ell)$ is a sufficient statistic for the hierarchy beyond~$\ell$ ---
    is proved at the level of the BFS rank observable for the uniform seed of the O12 pipeline
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff}), where the rank does not depend on the fibre label.
    Granting~\textup{[H-suff]}, the data-processing inequality~\cite{CoverThomas2006}
    implies that $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$ is non-increasing in~$\ell$.
    This result is \emph{not} a $c$-theorem; it does not count effective degrees of freedom.
    For the O12 three-character capacity under the sampling law of the O-series campaigns,
    the profile is invariant under multiplication of the whole character block by any
    unit~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a36}, and O32 deduces that its distribution does not depend on
    the label~\cite{Beau2026a36}.
    Hence $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$ at every depth, \textup{[H-suff]} holds for that observable,
    and the erasure bound reads $0 \leq 0$.
    This is independence in law, not equality of the profiles for each draw of the auxiliary
    characters.
    The theorem therefore proves no non-trivial erasure for the O12 observable: the label is
    absent from that observable, and no erasure mechanism is demonstrated.
    Version~2.0 corrects the rank-level proposition, removes attributions to the withdrawn O31
    results, and withdraws the reading of the O32 colour-triplet campaign as support for
    hypothesis~[H-color].
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
    \label{sec:intro}

    The ENI no-go theorem~\cite{Beau2026l} establishes a fundamental structural fact:
    genuine emergence is necessarily non-injective.
    Any surjective map $\Pi : \Omegaspace \to \Obs$ satisfying the projective-emergent
    conditions carries a strictly positive projection entropy~$S_\Pi > 0$:
    information about the microscopic representative is irrecoverably lost at the effective level.
    This is a qualitative result.
    It identifies the structure of information loss but says nothing about how this loss
    is distributed across a hierarchy of resolutions.

    The Cosmochrony spectral admissibility programme~\cite{Beau2026a1note} provides a concrete
    realisation of this hierarchy.
    For each prime~$q$, the BFS capacity profiles~$\sigma_c(\ell)$, indexed by
    Weil-block fibre label~$c$ and coarse-graining depth~$\ell$,
    track the residual projective capacity of block~$c$ as the BFS cascade advances.
    For the O12 three-character capacity, with the label as first character and the
    auxiliary characters drawn uniformly, the profile has the same distribution for every
    label at every finite~$q$~\cite{Beau2026a36}; differences between the mean profiles of
    distinct labels are finite-sample dispersion.
    The colour-triplet hypothesis $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]$, that the sectors
    $\{c, \omega c, \omega^2 c\}$ become indistinguishable for $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$,
    is not tested by that observable, which treats every set of labels alike; its O31
    derivation is withdrawn~\cite{Beau2026a35}, and nothing below rests on it.

    This paper addresses the following question:
    can the qualitative ENI no-go be connected to the BFS coarse-graining hierarchy of the
    capacity profiles by a principled information-theoretic structure?
    We show that the answer is yes, conditional on the structural sufficiency
    hypothesis~\textup{[H-suff]}:
    the residual fibre-information functional $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$
    is non-increasing in the coarse-graining depth~$\ell$,
    by the data-processing inequality~\cite{CoverThomas2006}.
    The result is conditional, not unconditional:
    \textup{[H-suff]} is proved at the rank level (Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff}),
    and for the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law it holds because the label is
    independent of the whole profile (Section~\ref{sec:o12-application}).
    In that application the conclusion is $0 \leq 0$; no non-trivial erasure is established.

    Two structural distinctions are essential before proceeding.
    First, the coarse-graining axis~$\ell$ is distinct from and orthogonal to
    the temporal cascade axis~$n$ of the PTO paper~\cite{Beau2026pto}:
    see Table~\ref{tab:axes} and Remark~\ref{rem:axes}.
    Second, as stated in Section~\ref{sec:claims}, ENI alone does not imply monotonicity;
    the hierarchical structure of~\textup{[H-suff]} and~\textup{[H-sg]} is
    the informational content this paper contributes beyond ENI.

    \subsection{Contributions and non-claims}
      \label{sec:claims}

      This paper establishes, conditional on hypotheses~\textup{[H-suff]}
      and~\textup{[H-sg]} (Section~\ref{sec:semigroup}):
      \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a monotone information functional $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$
for the BFS coarse-graining hierarchy (Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure});
\item its non-increase under depth-increment, via the data-processing inequality;
\item the characterisation of $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$ as label independence of the observable
(Corollary~\ref{cor:fixedpoint}), which for the O12 capacity holds already at depth~$0$ and is
not reached by erasure.
      \end{enumerate}
      This paper does \emph{not} establish:
      \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a $c$-theorem or analogue (no counting of effective degrees of freedom;
see open problem~\textup{[O-2]});
\item the capacity-level hypothesis~\textup{[H-suff]} for observables other than the O12
capacity under the campaign sampling law (proved at the rank level in
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff}; for the O12 capacity in law, see
Section~\ref{sec:o12-application});
\item the full capacity-level semigroup hypothesis~\textup{[H-sg]}
(proved at the rank level in Corollary~\ref{cor:rank-sg}, and there redundant;
the capacity-level residue is open, see~\textup{[O-1b]});
\item any non-trivial erasure for the O12 observable, for which the bound is $0 \leq 0$;
\item any statement about colour: the O12 observable does not distinguish colour triplets
from other sets of labels~\cite{Beau2026a36}.
      \end{enumerate}

      \paragraph{Strict disclaimer.}
        This result should not be called a $c$-theorem.
        It is a \emph{fibre-erasure theorem}:
        the monotone measures the loss of residual fibre-label information under
        admissible coarse-graining.
        A $c$-theorem-type statement would require an additional counting principle
        for effective degrees of freedom, which is not established here and is listed
        as open problem~\textup{[O-2]} in Section~\ref{sec:open}.

      \paragraph{ENI versus monotonicity.}
        ENI~\cite{Beau2026l} establishes that information is structurally lost under
        non-injective projection, but does not by itself imply any monotone hierarchy
        over the coarse-graining scale~$\ell$.
        The monotonicity of Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure} requires the additional
        hierarchical structure encoded in~\textup{[H-suff]} and~\textup{[H-sg]}:
        these are the informational content this paper contributes beyond ENI.

  \subsection{Programme placement}
    \label{sec:placement}

    The present paper occupies a hub position in the programme.
    ENI~\cite{Beau2026l} and Foundation~\cite{Beau2026m} provide the foundational input:
    the non-injective projection~$\Pi$, the Weil-fibre realisation,
    and the projection entropy~$S_\Pi$.
    The spectral admissibility programme~\cite{Beau2026a1note} provides the
    operational hierarchy: the BFS capacity profiles~$\sigma_c(\ell)$ and their
    numerical campaigns. O12~\cite{Beau2026a16} states that the profile is invariant under
    scaling of the whole character block by any unit, and O32~\cite{Beau2026a36} deduces from it
    that, under the campaign sampling law, the law of the profile does not depend on the label.
    This paper connects these two branches by translating the ENI projection
    entropy into the operational functional~$I(c;\sigma(\ell))$.
    The PTO paper~\cite{Beau2026pto} handles the complementary temporal axis~$n$
    (Table~\ref{tab:axes});
    the two papers are structurally orthogonal and together constitute
    the information-theoretic architecture of the admissibility cascade.

  \section{Framework}
  \label{sec:framework}

  \subsection{Non-injective projection and fibre structure}
    \label{sec:fibre}

    We recall the setting of~\cite{Beau2026l,Beau2026m}.

    \begin{definition}[Non-injective projection and fibre]
      \label{def:projection}
      A \emph{projective description} is a surjective map $\Pi : \Omegaspace \to \Obs$
      such that $\Obs$ carries no microstate-resolving structure independent of~$\Pi$.
      The \emph{fibre} over $o \in \Obs$ is
      $\Pi^{-1}(o) = \{\,\omega \in \Omegaspace : \Pi(\omega) = o\,\}$.
      The \emph{projection entropy} is
      $S_\Pi = \mathbb{E}_{o}[\,\log|\Pi^{-1}(o)|\,] \geq 0$,
      with $S_\Pi > 0$ whenever $\Pi$ is non-injective
      \textup{(ENI Theorem~2.1,~\cite{Beau2026l})}.
    \end{definition}

    In the spectral realisation~\cite{Beau2026m}, the fibre structure is implemented
    via the Weil representation $V_\rho$ of
    $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ for a prime~$q$.
    The candidate minimal non-injectivity is the parity involution $c \leftrightarrow q - c$,
    a structurally motivated hypothesis (O18 establishes Born--Infeld parity as a covariance
    of the response family, with character $(-1)^{k}$ in the derivative order, and states the
    fibre identification as an open problem): under it, Weil blocks
    $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$ map to the same effective observable class.
    The \emph{fibre label} $c \in \{1,\ldots,(q-1)/2\}$
    indexes blocks within an equivalence class under~$\Pi$.

  \subsection{The BFS coarse-graining hierarchy}
    \label{sec:bfs}

    \begin{definition}[BFS capacity profile]
      \label{def:bfs}
      For a prime~$q$, a fibre label~$c$, and BFS depth $\ell \in \mathbb{N}$,
      the \emph{BFS capacity profile} $\sigma_c(\ell) \in [0,1]$ is the normalised
      residual projective capacity of block~$c$ at depth~$\ell$ along the
      Heisenberg Cayley graph BFS cascade~\cite{Beau2026a16}.
      The \emph{pair observable} is
      $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(\ell) = \sigma_c(\ell)\cdot\sigma_{q-c}(\ell)$
      on conjugate pair $(c,q-c)$.
    \end{definition}

    For a prime~$q$, the Heisenberg group $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$
    carries a Cayley graph whose BFS shells~$B_\ell$ grow as $|B_\ell| \sim \ell^4$
    in the Heisenberg regime~\cite{Beau2026a16}.
    The capacity profile~$\sigma_c(\ell)$ measures the residual Gram-Schmidt span
    of Weil block~$\rho_c$ not yet exhausted at shell depth~$\ell$:
    it starts at $\sigma_c(0) = 1$ and decreases toward~$0$
    as BFS saturation is approached.
    The depth parameter~$\ell \in \mathbb{N}$ is discrete;
    the coarse-graining semigroup of Section~\ref{sec:semigroup}
    therefore acts on a discrete monoid, not a continuous scale.

    \begin{remark}[Cascade and coarse-graining are orthogonal]
      \label{rem:axes}
      The admissible cascade $U_0 \to U_1 \to \cdots$ defines the temporal axis~$n$
      and is \emph{not} an RG flow.
      RG-like behaviour is carried by~$\Pi$ and the semigroup $\{G_{\ell' \leftarrow \ell}\}$
      on the $\ell$-axis.
      The cascade \emph{enlarges} the admissible space as temporal ordering advances;
      an RG flow toward the IR \emph{reduces} effective degrees of freedom.
      These are distinct phenomena on orthogonal axes.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{The coarse-graining semigroup}
    \label{sec:semigroup}

    \begin{definition}[Coarse-graining channel]
      \label{def:channel}
      For $\ell' > \ell$, the \emph{coarse-graining channel}
      $G_{\ell' \leftarrow \ell} : \sigma(\ell) \mapsto \sigma(\ell')$
      is the map sending the BFS capacity profile at depth~$\ell$ to the profile
      at depth~$\ell'$.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{hypothesis}[\textup{[H-sg]}: semigroup property]
      \label{hyp:sg}
      The coarse-graining channels satisfy
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:sg}
        G_{\ell_3 \leftarrow \ell_2} \circ G_{\ell_2 \leftarrow \ell_1}
        = G_{\ell_3 \leftarrow \ell_1}
        \qquad \text{for all } \ell_1 < \ell_2 < \ell_3.
      \end{equation}
    \end{hypothesis}

    Hypothesis~\textup{[H-sg]} is plausible from the nested structure of BFS shells:
    the subgraph induced by~$B_{\ell_1}$ is contained in that induced by~$B_{\ell_2}$
    for $\ell_1 < \ell_2$,
    so the transition $\sigma(\ell_1) \to \sigma(\ell_2)$ can in principle be factored
    through~$\sigma(\ell_1)$ alone.
    Without~\textup{[H-sg]}, the data-processing inequality applied step-by-step
    via~\textup{[H-suff]} yields $I(c;\sigma(\ell+1)) \leq I(c;\sigma(\ell))$
    for each consecutive pair,
    but not the uniform statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure} for all $\ell' > \ell$.
    Note that the semigroup acts on the space of observable descriptions,
    not on the static substrate~$\Omegaspace$:
    composability is a property of the coarse-graining of effective observables.
    At the level of the rank observable~\textup{[H-sg]} holds and is moreover redundant
    (Corollary~\ref{cor:rank-sg}); only its capacity-level form remains hypothetical here.

  \subsection{Probability measure on fibre labels}
    \label{sec:measure}

    \begin{definition}[Fibre-label measure]
      \label{def:measure}
      Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $\{c : 1 \le c \le (q-1)/2\}$.
      Two canonical choices are used:
      \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Uniform}: $\mu(c) = 2/(q-1)$ for all~$c$.
\item \emph{Triplet-uniform} (for $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$):
$\mu$ uniform on $\{c,\,\omega c,\,\omega^2 c\}$,
where $\omega$ is a primitive cube root of unity modulo~$q$;
the orbit used by the O32 campaign~\cite{Beau2026a36}, for which the O12 observable carries
no colour content.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{definition}

    The choice of~$\mu$ does not affect the monotonicity of $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$
    (which holds for any fixed measure by DPI),
    but affects its value and the interpretation of the fixed point.

  \section{Structural Sufficiency and the Markov Property}
  \label{sec:suff}

  \begin{hypothesis}[\textup{[H-suff]}: structural sufficiency]
    \label{hyp:suff}
    For all $\ell' > \ell$, the coarse-graining channel $G_{\ell' \leftarrow \ell}$
    is conditionally independent of the fibre label~$c$ given $\sigma(\ell)$:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:markov}
      c \;\longrightarrow\; \sigma(\ell) \;\longrightarrow\; \sigma(\ell').
    \end{equation}
    That is, $\sigma(\ell)$ is a \emph{sufficient statistic} for~$\sigma(\ell')$
    with respect to the fibre label~$c$.
    The term ``sufficient'' is used in the information-theoretic sense:
    all information in~$\sigma(\ell)$ that is predictive of~$\sigma(\ell')$
    is already fully encoded; no additional knowledge of~$c$ reduces
    the uncertainty in~$\sigma(\ell')$ beyond what $\sigma(\ell)$ already captures.
  \end{hypothesis}

  \begin{remark}[Interpretive status of~\textup{[H-suff]}]
    \label{rem:suff-not-adhoc}
    Hypothesis~\textup{[H-suff]} is not introduced solely to enable the DPI.
    It is the informational reformulation of an effective closure property already
    implicit throughout the spectral admissibility programme:
    if~\textup{[H-suff]} failed --- if BFS dynamics at depth~$\ell'$ re-accessed
    label information not encoded in~$\sigma(\ell)$ ---
    then $\sigma_c(\ell)$ would not constitute stable effective observables under~$\Pi$.
    More precisely,~\textup{[H-suff]} is the condition that the BFS observable~$\sigma(\ell)$
    constitutes a \emph{complete summary} of the fibre structure at resolution~$\ell$:
    if it holds, no deeper BFS step retrieves label information that $\sigma(\ell)$ does not
    already carry.
    This is a hypothesis-level motivation, consistent with the non-injectivity of~$\Pi$
    (ENI~\cite{Beau2026l}), not a derivation: ENI does not imply~\textup{[H-suff]}, and no
    mechanism by which~$\Pi$ would remove label information along the hierarchy is established.
    For the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law,~\textup{[H-suff]} holds for a different
    reason: the label is absent from the observable at every depth, by the unit-scaling invariance
    of the profile and the uniform law of the auxiliary characters
    (Section~\ref{sec:o12-application}).
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Status of~\textup{[H-suff]}]
    \label{rem:suff-status}
    Hypothesis~\textup{[H-suff]} is proved at the rank level in
    Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff}.
    For the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law it holds in law, because the label
    is independent of the whole profile (Section~\ref{sec:o12-application}).
    A permutation-based Spearman test on BFS capacity data for $q \in \{61, 151, 211\}$
    yields no significant $c$-dependence in step-by-step residuals at any tested depth
    (Section~\ref{sec:hsuff-test}), as that result requires.
    For other observables, sampling laws or seeds, \textup{[H-suff]} remains a hypothesis.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Why~\textup{[H-suff]} is load-bearing]
    \label{rem:loadsuff}
    If hypothesis~\textup{[H-suff]} fails for some pair $(\ell,\ell')$ ---
    that is, if $\sigma(\ell')$ depends on~$c$ beyond its dependence on~$\sigma(\ell)$
    --- then no structural information monotone of the form
    $I(c;\sigma(\ell')) \leq I(c;\sigma(\ell))$ can be guaranteed.
    If $p(\sigma(\ell') \mid \sigma(\ell), c) \neq p(\sigma(\ell') \mid \sigma(\ell))$
    for some~$c$, then the chain $c \to \sigma(\ell) \to \sigma(\ell')$ is not Markov,
    and the data-processing inequality does not apply.
    This is not a limitation of the proof technique;
    it reflects the genuine logical dependence of the monotonicity on the Markov structure.
  \end{remark}

  A proof of~\textup{[H-suff]} requires showing that the BFS depth-increment kernel
  $p(\sigma(\ell+1) \mid \sigma(\ell), c)$ is independent of the fibre label~$c$.
  At the level of the BFS rank observable this is now established.

  \begin{definition}[BFS rank observable]
    \label{def:rank}
    Write Heisenberg elements as $(a,b,z)$ with horizontal part~$(a,b)$ and central part~$z$,
    and let $V_c$ be the representation space of the Weil block~$\rho_c$.
    For a prime~$q$, a fibre label~$c$ and a depth~$\ell$, with the uniform seed
    $u_0 = q^{-1/2}(1,\dots,1) \in V_c = \mathbb{C}^q$ used by the O12
    pipeline~\cite{Beau2026a16} and $\rho_c$ in the Schr\"odinger convention
    $(\rho_c(a,b,z)f)(x) = e^{2\pi i c(z + b(x-a))/q} f(x-a)$,
    the BFS rank observable is
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:rank-def}
      r_c(\ell) = \dim \mathrm{span}
      \bigl\{\, \rho_c(a,b,z)\, u_0 : (a,b,z) \in B_\ell \,\bigr\}.
    \end{equation}
  \end{definition}

  \begin{proposition}[Structural sufficiency at the rank level]
    \label{prop:rank-suff}
    For every odd prime~$q$, every fibre label~$c \neq 0$, and every depth~$\ell$,
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:rank-l1}
      r_c(\ell)
      = \#\bigl\{ b(g) : g \in B_\ell \bigr\}
      = \min(2\ell + 1,\, q),
    \end{equation}
    the number of distinct second coordinates reached by~$B_\ell$.
    The right-hand side does not depend on~$c$;
    hence $r_c(\ell)$ is identical for all fibre labels, $I(c; r(\ell)) \equiv 0$,
    and~\textup{[H-suff]} holds at the rank level for every label,
    not only within conjugate pairs.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    For the uniform seed,
    $(\rho_c(a,b,z)u_0)(x) = q^{-1/2}\, e^{2\pi i c(z - ab)/q}\, e^{2\pi i c b x/q}$:
    each vector is a unimodular multiple of the Fourier character $x \mapsto e^{2\pi i cbx/q}$.
    Since $c \neq 0$, distinct values of $b$ give distinct, hence orthogonal, characters,
    while elements with the same $b$ give proportional vectors.
    The rank is therefore the number of distinct values $b(g)$, $g \in B_\ell$.
    Each generator changes $b$ by at most one and the powers $Y^{\pm k}$, $k \leq \ell$, lie in
    $B_\ell$, so these values are the residues of the integers in $[-\ell, \ell]$, of which there
    are $\min(2\ell+1, q)$.
    Since $r_c(\ell)$ is the same deterministic function of~$\ell$ for all~$c$,
    it carries no information about the label, so $I(c; r(\ell)) = 0$ at every depth
    and the chain $c \to r(\ell) \to r(\ell')$ is trivially Markov.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Earlier statement and other seeds]
    \label{rem:rank-seed}
    Version~1.3 stated~\eqref{eq:rank-l1} as the number of horizontal cosets $(a,b)$ reached
    by~$B_\ell$, for any non-zero seed, on the ground that each coset contributes one
    direction.
    That step does not give linearly independent directions, and the statement is false for
    the uniform seed: at $\ell = 1$ the ball reaches five cosets but three values of~$b$, and
    the rank is three.
    For a seed other than~$u_0$ no formula is claimed here.
    As a numerical observation only, a random seed at $q = 13$ gave rank
    $\min(\#\text{cosets}, q)$ for $\ell \leq 4$; in every case $r_c(\ell) \leq q$.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{corollary}[Rank-level composability and redundancy of~\textup{[H-sg]}]
    \label{cor:rank-sg}
    On the pre-saturation window, where $\ell \mapsto r_c(\ell)$ is strictly increasing,
    the rank coarse-graining channel $G^{r}_{\ell' \leftarrow \ell} : r(\ell) \mapsto r(\ell')$
    is single-valued and composes,
    $G^{r}_{\ell_3 \leftarrow \ell_2} \circ G^{r}_{\ell_2 \leftarrow \ell_1}
    = G^{r}_{\ell_3 \leftarrow \ell_1}$ for all $\ell_1 < \ell_2 < \ell_3$,
    so~\textup{[H-sg]} holds at the rank level.
    Moreover~\textup{[H-sg]} is not needed at the rank level:
    since $I(c; r(\ell)) \equiv 0$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff},
    the inequality $I(c; r(\ell')) \leq I(c; r(\ell))$ holds for every pair $\ell' > \ell$
    directly, with no step-by-step lifting.
  \end{corollary}

  \begin{proof}
    By Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff} the value $r_c(\ell) = \min(2\ell+1, q)$ is strictly
    increasing in~$\ell$ until it reaches $q$ at $\ell = (q-1)/2$.
    On this window $\ell$ is recovered from $r(\ell)$, so $G^{r}_{\ell' \leftarrow \ell}$
    is the single-valued advance of the common deterministic trajectory;
    advancing from $\ell_1$ to $\ell_2$ and then to $\ell_3$ reaches the same value
    as advancing directly to $\ell_3$, which is the stated composition.
    The second statement is immediate from $I(c; r(\ell)) \equiv 0$ at every depth.
  \end{proof}

  Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff} supersedes the earlier pair-level observation that
  conjugate labels $c$ and $q-c$ behave alike, which is the special case of label-blindness
  restricted to a single orbit.
  It also replaces a spectral route, which is not available: no result of the corpus controls
  the capacity~$\sigma_c(\ell)$ through Weil-block spectra, the O31 spectral material being
  withdrawn or under re-audit~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
  The step from the rank to the capacity concerns the three-character fingerprint of the
  admissibility pipeline~\cite{Beau2026a16}, by which $\sigma_c(\ell)$ differs
  from~$r_c(\ell)$.
  For the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law this step is settled in law by O32
  (Section~\ref{sec:o12-application}); it was open problem~\textup{[O-1a$'$]}
  (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

  \section{The Fibre-Erasure Theorem}
  \label{sec:erasure}

  \subsection{The information functional}

    \begin{definition}[Residual fibre information]
      \label{def:info}
      The \emph{residual fibre information} at depth~$\ell$ is
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:mutual}
        I(c;\sigma(\ell))
        = H(c) - H(c \mid \sigma(\ell))
        = H(\sigma(\ell)) - H(\sigma(\ell) \mid c),
      \end{equation}
      where $H(\cdot)$ denotes Shannon entropy with respect to measure~$\mu$.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{remark}[Physical interpretation]
      \label{rem:info-interp}
      $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$ if and only if $\sigma(\ell) \perp c$:
      the BFS profile carries no information about the microscopic fibre representative.
      This is a statement of label independence; it says nothing about how, or whether, that
      independence is reached along the hierarchy.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Main theorem}

    \begin{theorem}[Fibre-Erasure Theorem]
      \label{thm:erasure}
      Assume hypotheses~\textup{[H-suff]} (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:suff})
      and~\textup{[H-sg]} (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:sg}).
      Then for all $\ell' > \ell \geq 0$:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:erasure}
        I\bigl(c;\sigma(\ell')\bigr) \;\leq\; I\bigl(c;\sigma(\ell)\bigr).
      \end{equation}
      In particular, $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$ is non-increasing in~$\ell$
      and converges to a limit $I_\infty \geq 0$.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By~\textup{[H-suff]}, the triple $(c,\sigma(\ell),\sigma(\ell'))$ forms
a Markov chain~\eqref{eq:markov}.
The data-processing inequality~\cite{CoverThomas2006}
applied to this chain gives~\eqref{eq:erasure} directly.
Composability~\textup{[H-sg]} extends the inequality from $\ell' = \ell + 1$
to all $\ell' > \ell$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Label-independence condition]
      \label{cor:fixedpoint}
      Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure}.
      Then the residual information vanishes, $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$, if and only if
      $\sigma(\ell) \perp c$.
      In that case the information also vanishes at every greater depth: from depth~$\ell$ on, the
      observable carries no fibre-label information.
      For the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law, $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$ holds already
      at $\ell = 0$ (Section~\ref{sec:o12-application}): this is absence of label information in
      the observable, not a state reached by erasure.
    \end{corollary}

    \paragraph{Explicit disclaimer.}
      Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure} is \emph{not} a $c$-theorem.
      When $I_\infty = 0$, the limit does not count any effective degrees of freedom.
      A $c$-theorem analog requires a separate counting functional;
      see open problem~\textup{[O-2]}.

  \subsection{Application to the O12 capacity}
  \label{sec:o12-application}

    For a label $c$, the O12 three-character capacity is computed on blocks $(c, c_2, c_3)$
    whose auxiliary characters are drawn independently and uniformly from
    $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^*$, draws with $c + c_2 + c_3 = 0$ being rejected; this is the
    sampling law of the O-series campaigns from which the data of
    Section~\ref{sec:numerics} are taken.
    The profile is invariant under multiplication of the whole block by any unit (O12's exact
    rank formula~\cite{Beau2026a16}; O32, Theorem~4~\cite{Beau2026a36}), and O32 deduces
    (Corollary~5) that, under this law, the distribution of the entire profile
    $(\sigma(\ell))_{\ell}$ is the same for every label.

    \begin{proposition}[Absence of label information in the O12 capacity]
      \label{prop:o12-zero}
      Let $\sigma$ be any function of the rank sequence of the O12 three-character fingerprint
      (in particular the normalised increment $\Delta r/|S|$ used by the campaigns).
      Under the campaign sampling law and for any label measure~$\mu$, the label $c$ is
      independent of the profile $(\sigma(\ell))_{\ell}$, and of the mean of any number of
      independently drawn profiles.
      Consequently $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$ for every~$\ell$, the chain~\eqref{eq:markov} is
      Markov, \textup{[H-suff]} holds for this observable, and~\eqref{eq:erasure} reads
      $0 \leq 0$.
      The same holds for the pair observable $\sigma_c\,\sigma_{q-c}$ computed from independent
      blocks.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The law of the rank sequence, hence of any function of it, does not depend
      on~$c$~\cite{Beau2026a36}, so the joint law of
      $(c, (\sigma(\ell))_\ell)$ is the product $\mu \otimes \nu$, with $\nu$ the common law.
      Means of independent profiles and products of independent profiles inherit this
      independence.
      Mutual information vanishes for independent variables, and the Markov property is
      immediate.
    \end{proof}

    Four qualifications fix the scope of Proposition~\ref{prop:o12-zero}.
    First, it is independence in law: for a single draw of auxiliary characters, the profiles
    of distinct labels need not coincide; scaling the whole block by a common unit is one
    sufficient condition for equality~\cite{Beau2026a36}, not a necessary one.
    Second, it concerns this observable and this sampling law; it says nothing about the
    capacity of other constructions, seeds or label laws, for which \textup{[H-suff]} remains a
    hypothesis.
    Third, it demonstrates no erasure: the label is absent from the observable at every
    depth, so Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure}, whose proof stands under its hypotheses, yields only
    the trivial bound in this application.
    Fourth, Definition~\ref{def:bfs} normalises $\sigma_c(\ell)$ to $[0,1]$ with
    $\sigma_c(0) = 1$, whereas the stored campaign profiles are $\Delta r_\ell/|S_\ell|$, which
    are not so normalised; the proposition holds for either, since both are functions of the
    rank sequence.

  \subsection{Information decomposition and programme connections}
  \label{sec:decomp}

  By the chain rule for entropy:
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:decomp}
    H\bigl(\sigma(\ell)\bigr)
    = H\bigl(\sigma(\ell) \mid c\bigr) + I\bigl(c;\sigma(\ell)\bigr).
  \end{equation}
  Here $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$ is the fibre-erasure functional (Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure}),
  monotone non-increasing in~$\ell$;
  $H(\sigma(\ell) \mid c)$ is the intrinsic entropy of the observable conditioned on
  the fibre label, and is the natural candidate for a counting monotone~\textup{[O-2]}.

  \begin{remark}[Connection to PTO and axis orthogonality]
    \label{rem:pto}
    The temporal monotone $I(n) = \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0) - \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$
    of~\cite{Beau2026pto} lives on the temporal axis~$n$ (Table~\ref{tab:axes}).
    The present $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$ lives on the coarse-graining axis~$\ell$.
    They measure distinct phenomena on orthogonal axes and should not be conflated.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Numerical Proxies and Spectral Programme Connections}
  \label{sec:numerics}

  \subsection{Inter-sector variance as proxy}

    \begin{definition}[Inter-sector variance]
      \label{def:variance}
      The \emph{inter-sector variance} at depth~$\ell$ is
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:variance}
        \mathcal{V}(\ell)
        = \mathrm{Var}_c\bigl(\sigma_c(\ell)\bigr)
        = \mathbb{E}_c\bigl[\sigma_c(\ell)^2\bigr]
        - \bigl(\mathbb{E}_c[\sigma_c(\ell)]\bigr)^2.
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    $\mathcal{V}(\ell) = 0$ is a necessary condition for $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$,
    but $\mathcal{V}$ is \emph{not} guaranteed to be monotone in~$\ell$.
    Non-monotone behaviour of~$\mathcal{V}$ would not contradict Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure};
    it would indicate that variance is an imperfect surrogate for~$I(c;\sigma(\ell))$.

  \subsection{Triplet variance over cube-root orbits}

    For $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, the \emph{triplet-restricted variance} is
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:tripvar}
      \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{triplet}}(\ell)
      = \mathrm{Var}\bigl(\sigma_c(\ell),\;
      \sigma_{\omega c}(\ell),\; \sigma_{\omega^2 c}(\ell)\bigr),
    \end{equation}
    averaged over cube-root orbits $\{c, \omega c, \omega^2 c\}$.

    \begin{remark}[The triplet variance carries no colour content]
      \label{rem:hcolor}
      $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]$ asserts
      $\sigma_c(\ell) = \sigma_{\omega c}(\ell) = \sigma_{\omega^2 c}(\ell)$
      for $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$; its O31 derivation is withdrawn~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
      For the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law, the mean profiles of distinct labels
      computed from independent draws are independent and identically distributed (O32,
      Corollary~6~\cite{Beau2026a36}); the O25 pipeline from which the present data are taken
      draws each label from its own random stream~\cite{Beau2026a29}, so the condition holds. Hence
      $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{triplet}}$ has the same law for a cube-root orbit as for any other
      three labels.
      O32 shows on this basis that its colour-triplet campaign could not test
      $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]$~\cite{Beau2026a36}; $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{triplet}}$ measures
      finite-sample dispersion, and the present paper draws no colour conclusion from it.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Numerical consistency check of~\textup{[H-suff]}}
    \label{sec:hsuff-test}

    Hypothesis~\textup{[H-suff]} was checked numerically on the BFS capacity data
    from the O25 pipeline~\cite{Beau2026a29}
    for $q \in \{61, 151, 211\}$.
    For each depth step $\ell \to \ell+1$,
    the $k$-nearest-neighbour regression residuals
    $e_c = \sigma_c(\ell+1) - \hat{f}(\sigma_c(\ell))$
    are computed from all $q-1$ block-averaged mean trajectories,
    where $\hat{f}$ is estimated without reference to~$c$.
    The Spearman correlation $|\rho(e_c, c)|$ is then computed
    and compared to a permutation null (500 shuffles of~$c$),
    yielding a right-tail $p$-value and a standardised $z$-score.
    Under~\textup{[H-suff]}, the residuals~$e_c$ should be independent
    of~$c$, and $|\rho|$ should be statistically indistinguishable
    from zero at every depth.

    with the permutation null throughout:

    These results show no $c$-dependence of the depth-increment dynamics beyond the capacity
    profile, as Proposition~\ref{prop:o12-zero} requires for this observable: the test is a
    consistency check of an exact statement in law, not independent evidence for
    \textup{[H-suff]}.

    \paragraph{Limitations.}
      The test is limited by the available data:
      the O25 pipeline~\cite{Beau2026a29} stores only the mean BFS capacity
      profile averaged over $M$~block samples per fibre label;
      per-block trajectories would enable a more powerful categorical test.
      No full O25 run is currently available for $q = 307$.

    \paragraph{Leave-triplet-out $\eta^2$ test.}
      The leave-triplet-out $\eta^2$ test (panel~C of Figure~\ref{fig:hsuff}) groups labels by
      cube-root orbit.
      Its pipeline summary gives, per prime, the number of depths with $p < 0.05$, an excess
      score over the number expected by chance, and a verdict:
      at $q = 61$, 3 of 10 depths, excess $2.5\sigma$, $z_{\max} = 2.03$;
      at $q = 151$, 6 of 37 depths, excess $3.1\sigma$, flagged, $z_{\max} = 3.07$;
      at $q = 211$, 4 of 49 depths, excess $1.0\sigma$, consistent with the null, $z_{\max} = 3.25$.
      Version~1.3 reported only $z_{\max}$ at $q \in \{151, 211\}$ and read these scores as a
      corroboration of~$[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]$; that reading is withdrawn.
      The mean profiles of distinct labels are independent and identically distributed (see
      Remark~\ref{rem:hcolor}), so no within-orbit correlation is expected, and O32 shows that
      the orbit grouping carries no colour content~\cite{Beau2026a36}.
      At $q = 211$ the excess is within what the number of depths explains.
      At $q = 151$, and to a lesser degree at $q = 61$, it is not explained here; the seeding of
      the pipeline is a candidate source that has not been examined.
      The scores are reported as measurements and are not used.

  \subsection{Axis disambiguation and finite-sample dispersion}
  \label{sec:axis-disambig}

  Version~1.3 asked whether the condition $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$ is approached as a limit in
  the coarse-graining depth~$\ell$ at fixed~$q$ or only as $q \to \infty$, and answered with
  the diagnostics below on the BFS capacity data for $q \in \{61, 151, 211\}$.
  For the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law the question is settled differently:
  the condition holds exactly at every depth and every~$q$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:o12-zero}).
  The diagnostics remain measurements of the mean profiles; their differences across labels
  are finite-sample dispersion of means of $M$ independent profiles, and they are not read as
  label structure.

  \paragraph{Coefficient of variation.}
    The scale-free inter-sector spread
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:cv}
      \mathrm{CV}(\ell)
      = \frac{\mathrm{std}_c\bigl(\sigma_c(\ell)\bigr)}
      {\mathrm{mean}_c\bigl(\sigma_c(\ell)\bigr)}
    \end{equation}
    equals the depth-independent constant $\mathrm{std}(A_c)/\mathrm{mean}(A_c)$ if the profiles
    differ purely by amplitude, $\sigma_c(\ell) = A_c\,\sigma^*(\ell)$; a constant CV does not
    conversely imply that form.
    The diagnostic describes the spread of the mean profiles; it is not read here as a test of a
    fixed point of the coarse-graining semigroup.
    The data show~$\mathrm{CV}(\ell)$ starting small at the fitting-window entry
    ($0.045$--$0.079$ across $q \in \{61, 151, 211\}$)
    and growing throughout the pre-saturation regime at all three primes
    (Figure~\ref{fig:o4}, left panels);
    no plateau or decrease toward zero is observed.
    Since the expected profile is common to all labels, this growth measures the relative
    sampling dispersion of the means as $\sigma$ decreases and blocks saturate at different
    depths; it does not bear on a fixed point of the semigroup.

  \paragraph{Amplitude-ratio test.}
    For 20 random pairs $(c, c')$, the coefficient of variation of the ratio
    $\sigma_c(\ell)/\sigma_{c'}(\ell)$ over depth measures shape differences
    beyond amplitude scaling.
    Table~\ref{tab:o4} shows this quantity decreasing with~$q$.

        211 & 0.062 & 0.025 \\

    The ratio-CV decreases from $q = 61$ ($0.156$) to $q \in \{151, 211\}$ ($0.065$--$0.062$).

    The labels share one expected profile at every~$q$, so the ratio-CV measures the sampling
    dispersion of the means; its $q$-dependence is not analysed here.

    \begin{remark}[Fibre information at finite~$q$; terminology]
      \label{rem:fixedpoint-thermo}
      Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure} and Corollary~\ref{cor:fixedpoint} hold for any fixed~$q$:
      their conclusion is that $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$ is non-increasing; they do not require
      the label-independence condition to be reached.
      Version~1.3 stated that a residual $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) > 0$ persists at finite~$q$.
      For the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law this is false:
      $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$ at every depth (Proposition~\ref{prop:o12-zero}).

      A terminological precision is warranted.
      The condition $I(c;\sigma) = 0$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:fixedpoint} is a condition on the
      information functional ---
      not a fixed point of the semigroup $\{G_{\ell' \leftarrow \ell}\}$,
      which has no non-trivial fixed point at finite~$q$
      (all profiles eventually reach $\sigma_c(\ell) \to 0$ by BFS saturation).
      For the O12 observable the condition holds at every depth, depth~$0$ included, because the
      law of the profile is common to all labels at every finite~$q$; it is not reached along the
      hierarchy. The large-$q$ limit of that common profile, which Conjecture~4.7
      of~\cite{Beau2026q5b} concerns, is a separate question that the present paper does not
      address.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Numerical evidence for~\textup{[O-2]}: conditional entropy monotonicity}
  \label{sec:o2-test}

  The decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp} identifies $H(\sigma(\ell) \mid c)$
  as a candidate counting monotone (open problem~\textup{[O-2]}).
  A numerical proxy is available from the O25 pipeline
  extended with squared-profile accumulators:
  for each pair $(c, q-c)$ and BFS depth~$\ell$,
  the Bessel-corrected intra-block variance
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:varintra}
    \mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell, c)
    = \frac{M}{M-1}
    \Bigl(
    \mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{block}}[\sigma_c(\ell)^2]
    - \mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{block}}[\sigma_c(\ell)]^2
    \Bigr)
  \end{equation}
  estimates the spread of $\sigma_c(\ell)$ across the $M$ independent block samples
  for fixed fibre label~$c$.
  Under a Gaussian approximation,
  $H(\sigma(\ell) \mid c)
  \approx \mathrm{mean}_c\bigl[\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell, c))\bigr]$,
  and its monotonicity reduces to that of the proxy
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:hintra}
    h_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell)
    = \mathrm{mean}_c\!\bigl[\tfrac{1}{2}\log\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell, c)\bigr].
  \end{equation}

  \paragraph{Result for $q = 61$.}
    On the BFS capacity data for $q = 61$
    (30 conjugate pairs, $M = 20$ block samples, fitting window~$[2, 7]$),
    $h_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell)$ is perfectly monotone non-increasing
    over the pre-saturation regime:
    zero violations over five consecutive depth increments,
    linear-trend $t$-statistic $-25.5$,
    and a reduction of the intra-block standard deviation
    by a factor $0.024$ (approximately $41\times$) over the window.
    Figure~\ref{fig:o2} shows $h_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell)$,
    the between-$c$ entropy proxy $h_{\mathrm{inter}}(\ell)$,
    and the normalised spread $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell)$.

    The normalised spread
    $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell)
    = \mathrm{mean}_c\bigl[\mathrm{std}_{\mathrm{intra}}/\bar\sigma_c\bigr]$
    increases over the same range ($0.46 \to 2.05$).
    This reflects the staggered-saturation artefact identified
    in Section~\ref{sec:axis-disambig}:
    at late depths, some of the $M = 20$ blocks reach $\sigma_c = 0$
    while others have not, inflating relative within-$c$ variance.
    It is not a violation of~\textup{[O-2]};
    the absolute proxy $h_{\mathrm{intra}}$ is the correct test statistic.

    \begin{figure}[h]
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../code/o2_h_intra}
      \caption{Numerical test of~\textup{[O-2]}: $H(\sigma(\ell)\mid c)$ monotonicity
      for $q \in \{61, 101\}$ (blue and orange respectively).
      \textbf{Left:} $h_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell)$, proxy for $H(\sigma(\ell)\mid c)$;
      both primes are perfectly monotone non-increasing in the pre-saturation window
        (shaded band), with $t = -25.5$ at $q = 61$ and $t = -33.9$ at $q = 101$.
        The larger pre-saturation window at $q = 101$ directly reflects the larger~$q$.

    These results constitute preliminary numerical evidence that
    $H(\sigma(\ell) \mid c)$ is monotone non-increasing,
    consistent with~\textup{[O-2]}.

  \paragraph{Result for $q = 101$.}
    On the BFS capacity data for $q = 101$
    (50 conjugate pairs, $M = 20$ block samples, fitting window~$[3, 10]$),
    $h_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell)$ is perfectly monotone non-increasing:
    zero violations over six consecutive depth increments,
    linear-trend $t$-statistic $-33.9$,
    and a reduction of the intra-block standard deviation
    by a factor $0.021$ (approximately $47\times$) over the window.
    The slope is $-1.009 \approx -1$,
    suggesting that $\log\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{intra}}$ decays near-linearly with BFS depth
    --- a geometric concentration rate to track at larger~$q$.
    $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{intra}}$ increases ($0.46 \to 1.45$) for the same
    staggered-saturation reason as at $q = 61$ (Section~\ref{sec:axis-disambig}).
    The signal is stronger than at $q = 61$ ($t = -33.9$ vs.\ $-25.5$,
    seven pre-saturation depths vs.\ five),
    confirming that the monotone behaviour is not a small-$q$ artefact.

  \paragraph{Status of the Gaussian proxy.}
    The quantity $h_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell)$ is not an exact Shannon entropy
    unless the within-$c$ distribution of $\sigma_c(\ell)$ is Gaussian.
    It should be understood as a variance-based entropy proxy.
    Its monotonicity is nevertheless meaningful: any unimodal within-$c$
    distribution whose scale concentrates with~$\ell$ has decreasing entropy
    under standard scale-sensitive estimators.
    Thus the present test probes concentration of the intrinsic observable
    spread, not a full non-parametric entropy estimate.

  \paragraph{Counting requirement.}
    A genuine counting monotone requires more than monotonicity alone.
    One must also show that $H(\sigma(\ell) \mid c)$ approaches a non-zero,
    stable, and interpretable value at large depth, with a controlled $q$-dependence.
    For the O12 observable $H(\sigma(\ell) \mid c) = H(\sigma(\ell))$ by
    Proposition~\ref{prop:o12-zero}, so the proxy above measures the spread of the common
    profile law.
    If this limiting value vanished identically, the functional would not count
    effective degrees of freedom in the sense required by a $c$-theorem analog.

  \paragraph{Role of the multi-$q$ extension.}
    With $q \in \{61, 101\}$ now confirmed, the remaining planned tests are $q \in \{151, 211\}$.
    They should determine whether the slope of $h_{\mathrm{intra}}$ stabilises near $-1$
    across~$q$.
    For the O12 observable $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) \equiv 0$, so decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp}
    reduces to $H(\sigma(\ell)) = H(\sigma(\ell) \mid c)$ and there is no decay of the fibre
    information to compare; such a comparison would require an observable in which the label is
    present at some depth.

  \section{Rank-Level Closure and Open Problems}
  \label{sec:open}

  The rank-level components of the original problems~\textup{[O-1]} (analytical proof
  of~\textup{[H-suff]}) and~\textup{[O-1b]} (the semigroup hypothesis~\textup{[H-sg]}) are closed.
  Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff} establishes~\textup{[H-suff]} for the BFS rank observable
  unconditionally: $r_c(\ell)$ is fibre-blind and $I(c; r(\ell)) \equiv 0$, so the chain
  $c \to r(\ell) \to r(\ell')$ is trivially Markov.
  Corollary~\ref{cor:rank-sg} shows that, on the pre-saturation window relevant to the capacity
  analysis, the rank channel composes and~\textup{[H-sg]} is in fact not needed at the rank level,
  the all-pair inequality holding directly.
  Problem~\textup{[O-1a$'$]}, the capacity-level residue of~\textup{[O-1]}, is settled in law
  for the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law (Proposition~\ref{prop:o12-zero}); the
  problems below are otherwise open.

  \begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[\textup{[O-1a$'$]} Fingerprint preservation of the rank kernel (settled in law for O12).]
Since the rank carries no fibre information (Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff}),
any residual $c$-dependence in the capacity $\sigma_c(\ell)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure}
must enter through the O12 three-character fingerprint by which $\sigma_c(\ell)$ differs from
$r_c(\ell)$~\cite{Beau2026a16}.
O12's exact rank formula identifies this fingerprint precisely: the increment's rank contribution
depends on the block label $c=(c_1,c_2,c_3)$ only through the projective pair
$(\alpha,\beta)=(B/A,C/A) \bmod q$, where $A=c_1+c_2+c_3$, $B=c_2+c_3$, $C=c_3$ are block-only
constants --- a structural property of the block label itself, not of the visited elements'
central coordinates.
The central coordinate is exactly invisible to the increment: it multiplies each fingerprint
vector by a non-zero unit-modulus scalar and changes neither its frequency nor its span
rank~\cite{Beau2026a16}, so it contributes nothing --- not amplitude, not shape --- to
$\sigma_c(\ell)$.
The task was the statement that the increment kernel does not depend on the label,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:bi-kernel}
  p\bigl(\sigma(\ell+1) \mid \sigma(\ell), c\bigr) = p\bigl(\sigma(\ell+1) \mid \sigma(\ell)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Under the campaign sampling law~\eqref{eq:bi-kernel} holds, because the label is independent
of the whole profile (Proposition~\ref{prop:o12-zero}).
The mechanism is the invariance of the profile under multiplication of the whole block by any
unit~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a36}: the pair $(\alpha,\beta)$ ranges over the same distribution for every
label, so it cannot carry label information in law.
This settles the question for general labels $c$, $c'$, not only within an orbit; the O31 claim
that it was closed within each colour orbit is withdrawn~\cite{Beau2026a35} and is not needed.
For a single draw of auxiliary characters the profiles of distinct labels still differ, and
the dependence of a block's decay on $(\alpha,\beta)$, in amplitude or in shape, remains a
property of individual blocks that the present paper does not analyse.
Earlier versions cited the rank-one colour covariance of O32 as evidence of shape
universality; O32 shows that this rank carries no such content~\cite{Beau2026a36}.

\item[\textup{[O-1b]} Fingerprint composability of the capacity channel.]
At the rank level, on the pre-saturation window relevant to the capacity analysis,
the coarse-graining channels compose and~\textup{[H-sg]} is redundant
(Corollary~\ref{cor:rank-sg}).
What remains is whether the three-character fingerprint keeps $\sigma(\ell)$ a sufficient
Markov state, so that the capacity channels compose as
$G_{\ell_3 \leftarrow \ell_2} \circ G_{\ell_2 \leftarrow \ell_1} = G_{\ell_3 \leftarrow \ell_1}$
for all $\ell_1 < \ell_2 < \ell_3$.
For the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law this question no longer carries the
erasure statement: $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$ at every depth, so the bound
$I(c;\sigma(\ell')) \leq I(c;\sigma(\ell))$ holds for every pair $\ell' > \ell$ directly, as
$0 \leq 0$, without composability.
Whether the capacity channels compose remains open as a property of the profile dynamics.

\item[\textup{[O-2]} $c$-theorem analog via $H(\sigma(\ell))$.]
The decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp} identifies
$H(\sigma(\ell) \mid c) = H(\sigma(\ell)) - I(c;\sigma(\ell))$
as a candidate counting monotone.
Establish its monotonicity in~$\ell$ and verify that its fixed-point value
counts effective degrees of freedom.
This would upgrade the fibre-erasure theorem to a genuine $c$-theorem analog.
Numerical evidence for the monotonicity is now available for $q \in \{61, 101\}$
via the intra-block variance proxy (Section~\ref{sec:o2-test}),
with a consistent slope ${\approx}{-1}$ at $q = 101$;
cross-$q$ confirmation at $q \in \{151, 211\}$ and an analytical proof remain open.

\item[\textup{[O-3]} Temporal causal structure and emergent light cone.]
Define the temporal mutual information
$I_{\mathrm{temp}}(n,k) = I(U_n;\,U_{n+k})$
and the transfer entropy
$T(U_n \to U_{n+k}) = I(U_{n+k};\,U_n \mid U_{n-1},\ldots,U_0)$.
Their decay in~$k$ would yield an operational definition of the emergent forward
light cone on the temporal axis~$n$, orthogonal to the present paper.

  \end{description}

  \section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

  This paper establishes a conditional monotonicity property for the BFS coarse-graining
  hierarchy on $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$:
  under the structural sufficiency hypothesis~\textup{[H-suff]}, and the semigroup
  hypothesis~\textup{[H-sg]} for the uniform all-pair statement, admissible depth-increment
  coarse-graining cannot increase the fibre information $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$.
  For the O12 capacity under the campaign sampling law, the hypotheses hold and the
  information is zero at every depth (Proposition~\ref{prop:o12-zero}): the label is absent
  from that observable, the bound is $0 \leq 0$, and no non-trivial erasure is shown.

  The central analytical contribution is the identification of a rigorous, unconditional
  partial closure.
  Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-suff} shows that, for the uniform seed of the O12 pipeline,
  the BFS rank observable is $r_c(\ell) = \min(2\ell+1, q)$, the number of distinct second
  coordinates in the ball, independent of $c$, so $I(c;r(\ell)) \equiv 0$ at every depth.
  Corollary~\ref{cor:rank-sg} shows that the rank channel composes trivially on the
  pre-saturation window and that~\textup{[H-sg]} is not even needed at that level.
  Both results follow from a single mechanism: each seed translate is a single Fourier
  character, of frequency $cb$ with $b$ its second coordinate; the frequencies depend on~$c$, but
  their number does not.
  At the capacity level, the O12 observable under the campaign sampling law satisfies
  \textup{[H-suff]} in law through the unit-scaling invariance of the profile
  (O12~\cite{Beau2026a16}) and the character-independent law deduced from it in
  O32~\cite{Beau2026a36}, which settles~\textup{[O-1a$'$]} for general labels
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:o12-zero}).
  This is independence in law, not equality of profiles for each draw of the auxiliary
  characters; how a single block's decay depends on its projective-frequency pair
  $(\alpha,\beta)$ is not analysed here.
  It carries no colour content: the O12 observable treats cube-root orbits like any other set
  of labels, and the earlier reading of O32's rank-one covariance and of the leave-triplet-out
  scores as evidence about $[\mathrm{H\text{-}color}]$ is withdrawn.

  For the candidate $c$-theorem analog~\textup{[O-2]}, the intra-block conditional-entropy
  proxy $h_{\mathrm{intra}}(\ell)$ is monotone non-increasing at $q \in \{61, 101\}$,
  with a consistent slope ${\approx}{-1}$ suggesting geometric concentration of the
  within-$c$ capacity spread.
  Tests at $q \in \{151, 211\}$ are planned.

  Two limits of interpretation follow.
  The rank carries no label information by construction: it depends only on the second
  coordinate of the ball. The O12 capacity carries none in law, under the campaign sampling law:
  it depends on the block only up to a common unit, the auxiliary characters are uniform, and the
  central coordinate is inert for this observable~\cite{Beau2026a16}.
  This is an absence of label information in the observables considered, at every depth,
  depth zero included; it is not a demonstrated erasure mechanism, and no depth at which
  information is lost has been exhibited.
  In particular, the condition $I(c;\sigma(\ell)) = 0$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:fixedpoint} does
  not, for these observables, support reading gauge invariance as the end state of an irreversible
  coarse-graining flow.
  A non-trivial application of Theorem~\ref{thm:erasure} requires an observable in which the
  label is present at some depth.

  In the Cosmochrony programme~\cite{Beau2026pto}, the conditional monotonicity complements
  the capacity-exponent results of the O-series~\cite{Beau2026a1note} and the
  quantum-geometric reconstruction of the Q-series.
  It concerns the resolution axis~$\ell$, while projective temporal ordering (PTO) concerns
  the temporal ordering of admissible projected capacity along the temporal axis~$n$.
